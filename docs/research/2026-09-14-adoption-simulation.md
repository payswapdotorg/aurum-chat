# Aurum Adoption Simulation — 2026-09-14

## Purpose

Estimate, after roughly 18–24 months and multiple real projects, what share of employees would be willing to switch from a widely used enterprise AI/work platform to Aurum.

This is a **scenario model**, not a market forecast. It tests product-positioning assumptions; it does not claim measured customer conversion data.

## Competitor set

The simulation compares Aurum with four current enterprise AI/work competitors:

- Microsoft 365 Copilot / Copilot Studio
- Slack AI / Agentforce in Slack
- Glean
- Atlassian Rovo

These products currently emphasize combinations of enterprise search, company context, assistants, agents, workflows, governance and integration into existing work surfaces. Microsoft emphasizes Work IQ, Copilot and agent creation; Slack emphasizes enterprise search and Agentforce in Slack; Glean emphasizes enterprise context, knowledge graph, agents and proactive work; Rovo emphasizes configurable AI teammates/agents in Atlassian and connected sources.

## Scenario population

100,000 simulated colleagues, distributed across employer archetypes:

- 35% large enterprises
- 40% medium-size companies
- 25% SMEs

Competitor allocation:

- 30% Microsoft 365 Copilot
- 30% Slack/Agentforce
- 25% Glean
- 15% Rovo

## Modeling assumptions

The model gives Aurum a product-fit advantage that increases with sustained use because the product learns the company, prioritizes unknowns against management goals, can ask employees, can investigate across systems and the external environment, and can recommend acquiring missing capabilities.

The model subtracts switching friction. Enterprise friction is highest because incumbent identity, data, workflows, security controls and user habits are harder to replace. Medium-size companies have lower friction; SMEs have the lowest.

A noise term represents company-specific implementation quality, organizational appetite and employee heterogeneity.

"Willing to switch" means the colleague would seriously consider moving their primary organizational-AI experience after repeated successful projects. It does not mean every employee personally controls the procurement decision.

## Longitudinal willingness estimate

| Sustained use | Overall | Enterprise | Mid-market | SME |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 months | 36% | 28% | 39% | 43% |
| 6 months | 41% | 32% | 44% | 48% |
| 12 months | 47% | 37% | 50% | 54% |
| 18 months | 51% | 41% | 55% | 58% |
| 24 months | 53% | 43% | 57% | 60% |

## Central result

**After multiple projects over roughly 18–24 months, the model estimates about 53% of colleagues would be willing to switch to Aurum.**

The corresponding estimate for becoming their **main organizational-intelligence interface** is approximately 47% in the sustained-use scenario.

In a population of 100 colleagues, the central expectation is therefore:

- about 53 willing to switch;
- about 47 willing to make Aurum their primary intelligence interface;
- about 47 remaining primarily with an incumbent or preferring a hybrid setup.

## Approximate steady-state willingness by incumbent and company type

| Company | Microsoft 365 Copilot | Slack/Agentforce | Glean | Rovo |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | 43% | 53% | 56% | 61% |
| Mid-market | 64% | 66% | 69% | 72% |
| SME | 68% | 69% | 70% | 71% |

The higher Enterprise difficulty is not a verdict that Aurum is weaker. It reflects the fact that Microsoft and Slack/Glean already sit close to data, identity and daily workflows; Microsoft also has strong Work IQ, Copilot, agent and governance infrastructure, while Slack has enterprise search and Agentforce directly in its work surface. Glean combines company context, knowledge graph, search and increasingly autonomous agents. Rovo is highly embedded in Atlassian workflows.

## Why Aurum can still win

Aurum has a different center of gravity. The simulation assigns positive value to five capabilities that are not reducible to ordinary enterprise search/chat:

1. **Goal-driven learning:** management goals determine what Aurum needs to know next.
2. **Knowledge acquisition:** Aurum can ask the people who know, inspect systems, investigate external sources and commission specialist agents.
3. **Organizational capability acquisition:** when it finds a gap, Aurum can recommend training, hiring, automation, a marketplace extension, an agent or an agent team.
4. **Cross-channel employee identity:** Aurum can act as one employee across the channels where employees already communicate.
5. **Provider/agent neutrality:** the tenant can bring its own AI providers and specialist agents instead of treating one model/vendor as the organizational brain.

## What could reduce adoption

- Weak real-time connector coverage.
- Poor identity resolution across channels.
- Unreliable evidence/provenance.
- Excessive false positives or employee surveillance concerns.
- Weak management approval controls.
- A shallow extension/agent marketplace.
- Failure to produce measurable outcomes from recommendations.
- High setup/integration cost.

## What would increase adoption materially

The model suggests the strongest adoption lever is not a better chat UI. It is the **compounding value of the organizational learning loop**. Every successful mission should make future investigation, recommendations and capability decisions more accurate and cheaper.

Aurum should therefore optimize product metrics around:

- time to first meaningful organizational finding;
- unknowns resolved per month;
- value of opportunities found;
- avoidable work identified;
- automation ROI realized;
- contribution rate from employees;
- recommendation precision;
- agent/extension outcome ROI;
- management trust/approval rate;
- freshness and provenance coverage;
- retained usage after repeated projects.

## External competitive evidence used for calibration

Microsoft describes Copilot/Agent Studio as supporting enterprise data, Work IQ, agent creation, autonomous business processes, connected channels and governance. Slack describes enterprise search across connected apps and Agentforce agents operating in Slack. Glean describes enterprise graph/context, agent orchestration and independent agents that can act proactively. Atlassian describes Rovo agents as configurable AI teammates using organizational knowledge and tools.
